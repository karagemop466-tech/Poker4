  • F20 '42 structure sheets audited' overstates the Venetian audit — data/structure-audit.json holds 42 records, but they mix Venetian DSE IV sheets with Wynn, Bellagio and other documents. tools/verify_poker4.py reports 12 tracked sheets with no record yet.   • F22 Wynn's $1,100 PLO structure sheet prints a fee split that does not add up ($975 + $85 + $45 = $1,105 against a $1,100 buy-in) — Structure booklet, WPT World Championship, Tuesday December 1, 2026, 1 p.m., $1,100 PLO. The row keeps the sheet's own wording and the three components verbatim; nothing is adjusted. It is excluded from the ranked list until the venue confirms whether the fee is $80 or the pool slice is $970.
  • F23 Bellagio's Saturday NLH sheet contradicts itself on price and prints a Day-2 date (9/13/26) that is already in the past — The PDF's title block says 'NLH $1100 BUY-IN', the Bellagio poker page lists 'SATURDAYS | $1,000 NLH', and rule 07 on the same sheet says 'Of the $600 Buy-in'. Its rule 04 also schedules the 'Combined Final Day 2 (9/13/26)'. The buy-in is therefore left blank, the row is not scheduled, and it needs a fresh sheet from the room before any money is committed.
  • F24 Wynn's daily slate is printed as a standing offer with no calendar dates, and the sheet itself says it may be suspended during other series — 'The Tournament will be offered Monday-Thursday and may be suspended during Seniors Events, Classic Events, or other Tournament Series.' Wynn runs its own WPT series from 2026-11-27 to 2026-12-21, so whether the $200/$240/$160 dailies run on any given trip date is not established by either document. Those rows carry no date and are never placed in the day plan.
  • F25 Caesars Palace's daily $100 slate is printed only in a marketing article: no house-fee split, no entry rule, no structure sheet — The Caesars page states four daily $100 NLH tournaments (10 a.m., 2 p.m., 6 p.m., 9 p.m.), 20,000 starting chips, a $1,000 guarantee, 20-minute levels and a $50 add-on for 15,000 chips. It prints no house-fee split and no re-entry rule, and the older Caesars/Horseshoe flyer that used to be linked now returns HTTP 404. The four rows are published with the printed prices, marked unverified for fee and entry rule, and excluded from ranking.
  • F26 Wynn prints its late-registration deadlines as blind levels, not clock times, so a clock-based fallback cannot be computed for those rows — Most WPT sheets read 'available until the start of level 9' and only some add '(approximately 4:30 p.m.)'. The clock figure is missing on the majority of the 69 sheets. Consequence: the day plan can only offer a fallback where the printed deadline is a time, which is why 13 of the 38 planned days carry no defensible second entry.
