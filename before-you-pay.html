The seven things to re-check

  1. The calendar for your dates. The schedule PDF is re-read every morning, but a printed series calendar is a snapshot of one series: check the room's own page for the day you travel.
  2. The structure sheet for each event you intend to enter. The sheet carries the buy-in, the fee split, the stack, the levels and the late-registration deadline — and, for the rooms that publish it, the entry rule. Read the sheet, not the summary.
  3. The entry policy, in writing. This is the single largest open item on the whole site: 0 of 253 published rows are confirmed single-entry. Ask the room for the maximum number of entries per player and the price of each subsequent entry, in writing, before you commit to a plan that depends on it (F1, Q1).
  4. The finish time. No official document on the Strip prints a finish time for these events, so the day plan sequences on registration deadlines only. Ask the room what time the event ends and what happens to your stack if it runs long (Q4).
  5. A dated checkout total for the exact stay. Advertised “from” rates are not quotes. Ask for the checkout total, the nightly average, the resort fee, the 13.38% room tax, parking and the cancellation deadline for your exact nights and occupancy (Q2, the hotel flags).
  6. Whether the poker-rate qualification applies to you. The advertised poker rate is conditional; if you do not qualify, the rate you actually pay is different (Q7).
  7. The walking route on the day. The walking times here are timestamped measurements from a routing engine, not statements by either property. Re-run the route on the morning you arrive (F3).
