Hotels: fee by fee, with the rate kept as a dated log

There is no single “price per night” on this site. Each observation records what was seen, on which date, on which official page, and whether it is a bookable quote or merely a published floor. The true nightly cost is computed the same way for everyone: (room rate + resort fee) × (1 + 13.38%), plus parking when you drive.

What could not be confirmed. Only two resort fees could be read on an operator's own page — The Venetian ($55), Wynn ($55) and Treasure Island ($49.95). No dated Wynn, Caesars or Bellagio room rate was readable, and Caesars Palace and Bellagio printed no fee at all: MGM's resort-fee and reservation pages both returned 404, and Caesars' property, hotel and policy pages printed none. Third-party figures exist for all of them and are deliberately not shown; they are listed as leads on Flagged for review.

Terms that change the total

  • The Venetian poker rate is conditional. $119/$209 are “from” figures for players who book by phone with code EPOKR25 and then play 6 hours of live cash per night, or buy into a $300+ tournament; miss it and the room reverts to the prevailing rate (suite-rates page). Add a $150/night incidental hold and an 11:00 a.m. checkout (hotel terms).
  • Parking. Venetian hotel guests pay $20/day self-park, but 3+ hours of poker play earns complimentary self-parking (parking page). Wynn and Treasure Island include self-parking. Caesars and Bellagio parking were not read, so they are blank.
  • Cancellation. Caesars advertises free 48-hour cancellation; Wynn's offer pages vary between a 48-hour deadline and fully non-refundable prepaid; the Venetian poker rate's deadline is not printed. Recorded per property in the rate log.

Walking distance to each poker room

One labelled OSRM distance matrix on OpenStreetMap data, converted with a single formula: minutes = metres ÷ 4.8 km/h × 60 × 1.25. The 25% allowance covers the Las Vegas Blvd crossings, pedestrian bridges, elevators and casino corridors — none of which the router models. Each row also shows the reverse route; where the two directions differ by more than 20% the row carries an asymmetric tag, because a large gap can be a real one-way pedestrian funnel or a geocoder snapping artefact, and quietly picking one number would hide it.

The 30-minute rule does bite — just not where you would guess. Six pairs exceed it: Wynn↔Caesars (42.4 min / 42.9 min), Wynn↔Bellagio (39.4 / 43.6), Treasure Island↔Bellagio (33.5) and Harrah's↔Bellagio (34.9). Every hotel is still within 30 minutes of at least one poker room, so no property is disqualified outright — but a Wynn guest cannot credibly walk to Bellagio between events.

Even the qualifying numbers are optimistic: they measure centroid to centroid and ignore the walk from your tower to the lobby, and the fact that the Venetian's poker room is on level 2 of the Grand Canal Shoppes while Wynn's is across in Encore.
